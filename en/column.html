Language & Culture

Notes on the small differences Korean–Japanese couples meet.

  • Messaging frequency is not a measure of love
  • How close to be with family?
  • When nuance gets lost

Messaging frequency is not a measure of love

Some people love frequent messages; others prefer to write only when needed. Neither is wrong.

What matters is not assuming that a slow reply means feelings have cooled. Tell each other early on when you are busy and how you feel about messaging. A small shared rule, like “one message every evening”, can help.

How close to be with family?

How people relate to their families differs more between households than between countries. Try putting into words what feels “normal” to you: how often you visit, how you keep in touch, which events you join.

When talking about each other’s families, share your own experience (“in my family we did this”) rather than comparing.

When nuance gets lost

Translation apps are useful, but jokes, indirect phrasing and the warmth of a message often get lost. If you sense a misunderstanding, switch from text to a call or video chat and hear each other’s voices.

Being able to ask “what did you mean just now?” without hesitation is a strong bridge across language.
